What makes an annuity bad? The red flags to watch for

By Gregory Stevenson · 2 min read

Most people ask me: "Are annuities good?" The question I wish more people asked is: "What makes an annuity BAD?" Because the honest answer is that a lot of annuities out there aren't great, and knowing the red flags is more valuable than hearing a pitch about the good ones. Here's what I look for.

Red flags worth knowing.

  • A surrender period longer than 10 years. Some products run 12 to 15. There's rarely a good reason for you to accept that.
  • Fees without a benefit attached. If there's an annual charge and nobody can explain exactly what it buys you, that's a problem.
  • "Bonus" rates that aren't free. Upfront bonuses are often paid for with a longer surrender period, lower caps, or a vesting schedule. Ask what the bonus costs.
  • A weak carrier. Anything rated below A- by A.M. Best deserves a hard look. You're trusting this company for decades.
  • Pressure. A deadline, a "rates change Friday," a push to sign in the first meeting. Good products survive a week of thinking.
  • All of your money. If someone wants to move 80% or more of your savings into a single annuity, walk away. It's a tool for a portion, not the whole plan.

The question that cuts through it.

"Why this product, for me, instead of the alternatives?" A good answer is specific: your income gap, your timeline, your need for liquidity. A bad answer is a feature list.
