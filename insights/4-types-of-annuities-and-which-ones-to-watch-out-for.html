The 4 types of annuities (and which ones to watch out for)

By Gregory Stevenson · 2 min read

"Annuity" is where most of the confusion lives, because the word covers several very different products. There are really four categories that matter.

1. SPIAs (Single Premium Immediate Annuities)

You hand over a lump sum, and income payments start within 30 days. Could be for a set period (say, 10 years) or for the rest of your life. Simple. Predictable. One of the oldest types around.

2. DIAs (Deferred Income Annuities)

Same concept as a SPIA, except the income doesn't start right away. You fund it now, and payments kick in at a future date — maybe 5, 10, or 15 years from now. This is useful if you're still a few years from needing income.

3. Fixed Annuities (including MYGAs)

These work a lot like CDs — you allocate your premium, and the insurance company credits a fixed interest rate for a set period. A Multi-Year Guaranteed Annuity (MYGA) holds that rate for multiple years. You know exactly what you're getting.

4. Fixed Indexed Annuities (FIAs)

These are where things get more interesting. Your growth potential is linked to a market index — like the S&P 500 — but your principal is protected from market losses. If the market goes up, you participate in some of that growth (subject to caps and spreads). If the market drops, you don't lose what you've already earned.

The ones to approach with caution

  • Variable annuities expose you to market risk directly. Your account value can go down. They also tend to carry higher fees.
  • RILAs (Registered Index-Linked Annuities) offer a buffer against losses but don't protect your principal completely.

Neither of those is inherently bad — but they require a different risk tolerance and a very clear understanding of the trade-offs.

The takeaway? "Annuity" is not a single product. It's a toolkit. And picking the right tool for the job is what matters.
