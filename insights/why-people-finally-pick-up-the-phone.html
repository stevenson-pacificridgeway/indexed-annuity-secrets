Why people finally pick up the phone

By Gregory Stevenson · 2 min read

I've talked with hundreds of people about their retirement, and I've noticed something about the ones who actually take action. It's almost never because they found the "perfect time."

What actually gets people moving.

  • The first statement after a bad quarter. Watching real dollars disappear from the account you're planning to live on changes the conversation from theory to personal.
  • A friend's story. Someone they know ran short, or someone they know set up income and stopped worrying. Either one lands harder than any article.
  • A birthday that ends in a zero or a five. Sixty. Sixty-five. The number on the cake makes "someday" feel like a date.
  • A spouse asking "what's the plan?" Often the most effective trigger there is.
  • A letter from HR or Social Security. A retirement date suddenly has paperwork attached to it.

Notice what isn't on that list: "I finally understood every detail." Nobody waits for that, because it never comes. They act when the problem becomes concrete.

Why this matters.

Every one of those triggers tends to arrive after some of the best options have narrowed — after a down year, after a claiming decision, after a rate change. The people who are happiest with their plan are the ones who made the call before the trigger forced it.

I'm not writing this to manufacture urgency. I'm writing because I've watched this pattern for years, and the only difference between the people who act and the people who wish they had is a phone call that took 15 minutes.
