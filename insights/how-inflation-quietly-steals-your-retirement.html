How inflation quietly steals your retirement

By Gregory Stevenson · 2 min read

Nobody wakes up one morning and says, "Inflation just cost me $30,000." It doesn't work that way. It works slowly — a little more at the grocery store, a bump in property taxes, a prescription that used to cost $40 and now costs $65.

Year by year, it feels manageable. Over a 20- or 30-year retirement? It adds up to a very different picture.

Let me put numbers on it

If you need $5,000 per month to live comfortably today, and inflation averages just 3% per year:

  • In 10 years, you'd need about $6,720/month for the same lifestyle
  • In 20 years, that number climbs to roughly $9,030/month
  • In 30 years? Over $12,100/month

Your expenses more than doubled, but your Social Security only adjusts partially through cost-of-living increases. And if any of your income is fixed — truly fixed — it's losing purchasing power every single year.

I've seen clients who started retirement comfortably and found themselves cutting back by year 10. Not because they made bad decisions, but because they planned for today's costs with today's dollars.

An example

A client came to me with about $100,000 originally earmarked for a conservative account. We explored how a portion could be positioned for potential growth over time — not to chase returns, but to help keep pace with the rising cost of living. That modest starting point became a much more meaningful part of their overall strategy as the years went on.

The point isn't that growth solves everything. It's that having part of your money positioned for potential appreciation — while another part provides stable income — may give you a better chance of keeping up.

Inflation isn't dramatic. That's what makes it dangerous.

It doesn't take your money all at once. It takes a little every year until you notice the gap between what you have and what you need. Planning for it now — while you have the most options — is worth the conversation.
