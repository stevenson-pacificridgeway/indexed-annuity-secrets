The $65,000 question: filling your retirement income gap

By Gregory Stevenson · 2 min read

Here's a number that surprises most people I work with: the gap between what they need each month in retirement and what they have coming in from reliable sources.

I call it the "income gap." And until you know yours, it's hard to make a confident plan.

How it works

Let's say you and your spouse need about $6,500/month to maintain your current lifestyle — housing, food, healthcare, a little travel, the basics. That's $78,000 a year.

Now, add up your reliable income sources:

  • Social Security: ~$2,800/month
  • Any pension: $0 (most people)
  • Other fixed income: varies

If Social Security is your only reliable source, you've got a $3,700/month gap. That's $44,400 a year you need to pull from somewhere.

Where does the gap money come from?

Most people default to withdrawing from their portfolio. And that works — for a while. But it introduces a risk most people don't think about: what happens if the market drops 25% in your first year of retirement?

That early loss, combined with ongoing withdrawals, can permanently reduce your portfolio's longevity. This is called sequence-of-returns risk, and it's one of the biggest threats to a retirement income plan.

An alternative approach

What if you could cover some or all of that income gap with a source that isn't dependent on market performance?

That's the role an income annuity can play. You allocate a portion of your savings to an annuity, and it converts that lump sum into monthly income — potentially for the rest of your life. The remaining portfolio stays flexible for growth, emergencies, and legacy goals.

The exercise

Take 5 minutes and do this math for yourself:

  1. What do you need each month? (Be honest. Include healthcare.)
  2. What reliable income do you have? (Social Security, pension, rental income)
  3. What's the gap?

That gap number is the most important number in your retirement plan. And once you know it, the conversation about solutions gets much clearer.
